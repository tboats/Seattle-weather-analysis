📐 Why both cos(ωt) and sin(ωt)? And why does A₂ represent Asymmetry?

1. Free Phase via Linear Regression

By the harmonic identity: A cos(ωt - φ) = a cos(ωt) + b sin(ωt). Fitting amplitude A and phase φ directly is non-linear and requires numerical approximations. Expanding into orthogonal cos and sin basis functions makes regression strictly linear, yielding an analytical closed-form solution with zero local minima.

2. The Role of Phase φ₁: Thermal Lag

A pure cosine without phase would lock its maximum to Jan 1 (t=0). The phase shift φ₁ = -161.5° rotates the peak forward to July 21 (for highs) and July 29 (for lows). This phase angle physically quantifies the ~30–34 day thermal lag of the Pacific Ocean and Puget Sound behind the summer solstice.

3. The Role of A₂ & φ₂: The Asymmetry Engine

A single sinusoid is strictly symmetric (182.5 days warming, 182.5 days cooling). The 2nd harmonic oscillates twice a year (T = 182.6 d). With A₂ = 2.52°F and phase φ₂ = 83.8°, it flattens the spring heating rate (+0.25°F/day) and accelerates the autumn cooling rate (-0.35°F/day). Autumn cools 1.36× faster than spring warms!

4. Sunlight Derivative vs. Thermal Lag

Daylight change (dD/dt) peaks precisely at the astronomical equinoxes: +3.82 min/day at the Spring Equinox (Mar 20) and -3.74 min/day at the Autumn Equinox (Sep 23). Comparing this against daily temperature change (dT/dt) demonstrates rate-space thermal lag: peak daily warming doesn't arrive until May 23 (a 64-day lag behind solar forcing!), as the marine heat sink buffers incoming radiation.

Seattle winter temperatures showed an exceptionally strong warming anomaly (+3.66°F Lows, +2.61°F Highs). Southerly Pacific storm tracks and marine cloud cover prevent radiational cooling during long winter nights.

💡 Meteorological Insights & Verification Lessons

Why Raw Correlation Fails in Weather Forecasting

Seattle temperatures naturally drop from summer into fall (August normal high: 76°F → October: 60°F). Any model that simply tracks the calendar shows a high correlation (r ≈ 0.85–0.95) from seasonal drift alone. At Day 7, AccuWeather has raw r = 0.685, but its Anomaly Correlation is only 0.074, proving near-zero skill beyond seasonal normals.

The Puget Sound Marine Inversion: Why NWS Dominates Lows

On Day 0 overnight lows, NWS achieves an outstanding 0.94°F MAE (r = 0.920) compared to ~2.6°F MAE for commercial models. NWS human forecasters and regional models accurately resolve Puget Sound marine air intrusions and nighttime radiational inversions that global grid models smooth over.

The 5-Day Predictability Horizon in the Pacific Northwest

Mean Squared Skill Score (MSSS vs. 30-year climatology) remains positive through Day 4–5, then crosses zero into negative territory by Day 6. Beyond Day 5, an average resident would get a more accurate forecast by simply looking up the historical climate normal for that day.

Systemic Warm & Cool Biases

Across almost all providers and lead times, Seattle forecasts exhibit a persistent warm bias on daytime highs (+1.5°F to +4.6°F) and a cool bias on nighttime lows (-0.5°F to -1.5°F). Commercial models systematically overestimate the diurnal temperature range in the maritime climate.
